Getting help

You do not have to close these gaps alone. Depending on who you are, the following no-cost or state-provided resources can help:

Washington state agencies — WaTech Office of Cybersecurity

  • Report a cybersecurity incident: 360-407-8800 · SOC@watech.wa.gov (Security Operations Center / CIRT)
  • Security design review for new or updated systems: sdr@watech.wa.gov
  • Cybersecurity risk assessments: RiskManagement@watech.wa.gov

Local governments, schools, tribes & critical infrastructure — Washington Cybersecurity Incident Response Team (WA CIRT)

  • A volunteer force of vetted cybersecurity professionals, operating under the Washington Military Department's Emergency Worker Program, that assists local governments, school districts, tribes, public utilities, and critical infrastructure before, during, and after cyber incidents — at no cost.
  • Learn more or request assistance: mil.wa.gov/cyber-security-program

Free national resources for government entities

  • MS-ISAC — free membership for state, local, tribal & territorial governments: advisories, Albert network monitoring, incident support (cisecurity.org/ms-isac)
  • CISA Cyber Hygiene — free external vulnerability scanning and assessments (cisa.gov/cyber-hygiene-services)
  • Report cybercrime: FBI IC3 (ic3.gov) · CISA incident reporting (cisa.gov/report)

Methodology

Thirty-three questions condensed from the 22 categories of NIST CSF 2.0, scored on the CSF implementation tiers (1 Partial, 2 Risk Informed, 3 Repeatable, 4 Adaptive). Category scores are the mean of their questions; function scores are the mean of their categories; the overall score is the mean of the six functions. Tier labels are floored, never rounded up. N/A answers are excluded from averages.

This self-assessment is a point-in-time snapshot based on self-reported answers; it is not an audit and does not certify compliance with any standard. Re-run it annually (or after major changes) and compare scores over time.
